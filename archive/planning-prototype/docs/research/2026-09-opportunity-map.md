# Opportunity map beyond Bonsai

Research checked 2026-09-27. “Uncharted” here means a **testable intersection that this search did not find directly demonstrated**. It does not mean first-ever, patentable, or publication-ready. The repo still has no benchmark results. The aim is to choose a real bottleneck rather than attach an FPGA to a fashionable model.

## The new constraint: action fidelity before speed

The newest work makes the project question sharper. [AD-WM (24 September 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30264) shows that low factual prediction error and even whole-bank action ranking need not track closed-loop success; its CEM-aligned elite regret was more informative in its experiments. [CoCo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04653) shows that action-conditioned video models can exploit visual inertia and produce futures weakly dependent on actions. [A counterfactual driving study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11601) finds that plausible direct alternative-action prediction can fail to preserve episode-specific evidence. These are distinct failure modes. An accelerator can make the *wrong counterfactual comparison* faster. This is the strongest reason to measure first-action regret and closed-loop success alongside throughput.

## Candidate directions, with adversarial checks

| Candidate | Narrow question and possible mechanism | What already exists | First experiment that could kill it | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Decision-sensitive numerical precision** | Can precision be spent on predictor states/steps that can change the winning action, while other computations run cheaply? A hardware version could rerun only ambiguous finalists at higher precision. | [QuantWM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02110) documents planning failures under low-bit DINO-WM; [mixed-bit DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11882) studies module allocation; [HAQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.08886) is earlier hardware-aware mixed precision. The [COMS 6998 menu](https://wan-research-group.github.io/coms6998-f26/project.html) explicitly proposes decision-preserving world-model quantization. | Freeze model and planner; log top-candidate margins and action flips at several precisions. If cheap margin tests fail to identify harmful flips or rerun cost exceeds savings, stop. | **Promising mechanism, crowded framing.** Differentiate through online selective recomputation and measured board-level benefit, not “decision-preserving quantization” alone. |
| **B. Counterfactual-fidelity-aware accelerator** | Before searching deeper, test whether changing the candidate action changes the predicted future in the correct direction. Could a tiny monitor suppress untrustworthy search and request a conservative policy or a different model? | [AD-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30264) and [CoCo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04653) already improve action discrimination at the model level. [Speculation/correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17250) already gates reuse from observation mismatch. | Using same-state multi-action simulator pairs, check whether a cheap monitor predicts *planning regret* better than prediction error, ensemble variance, or a fixed threshold. If not, a hardware monitor adds cost without value. | **High scientific importance, higher model/data risk.** A runtime decision interface is a possible systems angle, not yet an established hardware gap. |
| **C. Planning-aware latent memory/dataflow** | Can a scratchpad retain weights and frontier states across repeated candidate/horizon evaluations, and can it beat ordinary batched inference after all I/O? | [FPGA MPPI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17231) already exploits pipeline parallelism. [CPU–FPGA MCTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11208) already accelerates tree operations. The [COMS 6998 menu](https://wan-research-group.github.io/coms6998-f26/project.html) proposes a memory architecture for batched world-model planning. | Profile a real model: if weight reads dominate and frontier state reuse is tiny, or if the model cannot fit the target memory, a custom tree store has little payoff. Compare against a competent GEMM mapping. | **Most directly HPML and feasible after profiling**, but novelty requires a demonstrated cross-step reuse pattern or dataflow advantage. |
| **D. Event- or contact-triggered model resolution** | Could a small fast model predict most smooth motion and invoke a more detailed predictor near contact/mode switches? This changes *when* expensive dynamics are needed, not just how many branches to search. | [StructVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12553) already predicts sparse kinematic milestones. Contact is a known critical variable in [contact-conditioned control](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00776). Adaptive horizons are also established. | In a contact-rich simulator, segment decisions by contact proximity; measure whether a cheap event predictor identifies the steps where coarse dynamics changes action quality. If errors are not concentrated there, no special architecture is justified. | **Interesting but algorithm heavy.** Best considered only with a contact-rich task and labeled mode changes. |
| **E. World model of the accelerator design process** | Model HLS schedule transformations as state/action transitions and use predicted synthesis outcomes to choose which design to measure next. | [Compiler World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09312) already models tensor-schedule trajectories; [PatternDSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15068) prunes HLS schedules; [DiffAxE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10303) explores accelerator designs generatively. | Collect a small real HLS design space. Compare against random, pattern rules, Bayesian optimization, and a static cost model on *held-out kernels*. If sequence-aware prediction does not improve best design per synthesis hour, stop. | **Separate project.** It may be useful, but it is not a world-model planning accelerator and should not be combined with A–D. |

## What this search ruled out as a headline

- **“First FPGA world-model inference.”** [WMHA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16244) exists, and a recent [FPGA probabilistic recurrent predictor for plasma control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23141) reports deterministic sub-10 μs single-step inference. Neither solves the proposed planning problem, but the general headline is unavailable.
- **“First latent search or action chunks.”** [Dream and Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09832), [ELAST](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/6af779991368999ab3da0d366c208fba-Abstract-Conference.html), and [L-MAP](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/9a419ce12db8db2e35ee68c1f58c1a36-Abstract-Conference.html) cover these ideas.
- **“Deadline-aware planning” alone.** It is a named [course project direction](https://wan-research-group.github.io/coms6998-f26/project.html) and has older adaptive-MPC precedents. A paper needs a narrower implementation insight.
- **“Better visual prediction means better planning.”** [DINO-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04983) and [V-JEPA 2-AC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985) demonstrate latent planning, but [AD-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30264) and [QuantWM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02110) show why decision quality must be evaluated directly.

## Broader world-model frontiers

These are worth watching even if the project drops its FPGA emphasis. They require model or dataset work and are therefore weaker fits for a short hardware project.

| Scientific question | Evidence that the problem is active | A narrow, falsifiable angle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Does a model understand the effect of an intervention, or just continue a plausible scene?** | [CoCo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04653), [AD-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30264), and the [driving counterfactual study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11601) identify different failures of action controllability and counterfactual validity. AD-WM is a very recent preprint; treat its large reported gains as unreplicated. | Construct paired same-state, different-action cases with simulator ground truth. Compare factual error, action-response fidelity, elite regret, and closed-loop success. The underexplored part would be finding which *cheap* diagnostic predicts failure under a deployment budget. |
| **What must a compact world state remember when objects disappear?** | [WorldLines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18847) reports partial-observation and overwritten-state failures in long-horizon embodied tasks. [WROP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28654) introduces controlled object-permanence tests for video models. | Test whether an explicit tiny object-state memory improves action choice under occlusion at a fixed compute/memory budget, rather than scoring visual continuity alone. This is a model/memory study, not automatically an FPGA paper. |
| **Should predictive detail concentrate near physical events?** | [StructVLA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12553) already targets sparse kinematic milestones; dense future prediction can waste work and drift. | Compare fixed-rate latent prediction with event-triggered resolution near contact or mode changes, holding compute fixed and measuring control outcome. Distinguish true event prediction from oracle event labels. |
| **Can a model learn where its own imagined futures are unusable?** | [QuantWM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02110) shows planning failure under aggressive compression; [AD-WM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30264) shows factual error can mislead about planning utility. | Train or calibrate a low-cost *decision failure* predictor on rollout traces, and measure intervention precision and missed bad decisions under distribution shift. This is different from predicting average latent MSE. |

The important distinction is between a **problem** and a **contribution**. Counterfactual validity, memory, and adaptive detail are active research areas. The opportunity lies in a precise mechanism and comparison that survives the closest papers, not in naming the area.

## Recommendation for the next research pass

Keep **C (memory/dataflow)** as the hardware anchor and **A (decision-sensitive precision)** as the strongest alternative. Do not commit to either architecture yet. The next useful artifact is a small *decision trace* from an existing world-model planner: candidate scores/actions, prediction shapes and times, precision variants, action flips, and closed-loop outcomes. One trace can answer whether repeated inference is compute-, memory-, or scheduling-bound and whether low precision changes the choice. Then choose **one** mechanism with a baseline it can beat.

For a visual-planning pilot, the [JEPA-WMs study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24497) is a useful starting point because its [official repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/jepa-wms) releases code, data, checkpoints, and DINO-WM/V-JEPA-2-AC baselines. It also studies how architecture, training objective, and planner choice affect success, reducing the risk of mistaking a weak baseline for a hardware opportunity. For a compact continuous-control pilot, [TD-MPC2](https://github.com/nicklashansen/tdmpc2) remains a simpler planner to profile. These are alternatives; do not combine their results into one performance claim.

The core experiment should compare matched task success and deadline misses, not just model FLOPs. If board access is absent, an honest software-and-synthesis study may be more defensible than claiming an FPGA energy result from estimated operations.
